\section{What do we want a model for?}
There are three linked questions we cannot answer from the data and econometrics only:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Decomposition of gains: redistribution versus aggregate increases
  \item From that, net program effect on production
  \item Accurate cost benfit analysis under no-program counterfactual
\end{itemize}
\vspace{1em}
A spatial equilibrium model could deliver these.

\section{Proposed Model}
Environment:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Investors with farm projects originate from a nonproductive region O
  \item They choose where to install their farm on any of N productive regions
  \item Moving costs are increasing in the distance from the region to O 
  \item Productivity is local and match specific
  \item Farms rent land and capital
  \item Land markets are locally determined; the capital market is nationally integrated  
  \item The program acts by making local capital more available
  \item Farms produce a single good at a fixed unit price
  \item Investors choses where to form a farm (or not to invest) and how much to produce
\end{itemize}
\vspace{1em}

So that the investor solves:
\begin{align}
\max_{K_{i,n}, L_{i,n}, n} \qquad A_n\eta_{i, n}K_i^\alpha L_{i, n}^\beta - q_n K_i - r_n L_{i, l} - m(d_n)
\end{align}
Given a location choice, the firm choses L and K to maximize profits. This yields input demands:
\begin{align}
    \text{[K]} \qquad &  K_{i, n}^* = \alpha \frac{Y^*}{q_n}\\
    \text{[L]} \qquad & L_{i, n}^* = \beta \frac{Y^*}{r_n}
\end{align}
Such that optimal output and profit before moving are
\begin{align}
    Y_{i,n}^* =& [A_n\eta_{i, n}\alpha^\alpha \beta^\beta q_n^\alpha r_{n}^\beta]^\frac{1}{1-\alpha-\beta}\\
    \pi_{i, l} =& (1-\alpha-\beta)Y_{i,l}^*
\end{align}
Notice we get these due to decreasing returns to scale in factors.
This can be ascribed to firm owners collecting profits from firm-owned inputs.

Firm \(i\) location \(n_i\) is then decided by solving
\begin{align}
  n_i = max_{n \in N}{0, \pi{i, n} - m(d_n)}
\end{align}
Where the zero stands for not starting a firm at all.

Land supply is fixed exogenously for every region. 
Land markets are cleared by local land rents \(r_n\).

The capital market is national, cleared by the rate \(q\). Capital supply is given by
\begin{align}
  S_K(q) = K_{ref} * (q / q_{ref})**\epsilon.
\end{align}
Where \(\epsilon\) is the national capital supply elasticity, and \(K_{ref}\) and \(q_{ref}\) are reference values.
Setting it to zero yields a fixed capital supply; sufficiently high values yield a fixes interest rate.

The policy works via a interest rate subsidy. Let \(T_n \in {0, 1}\) indicate treatement for region n.
and \(s \in (0, 1)\) be the discount the program grants. Then,
\begin{align}
  q_n = q(1-sT_n)
\end{align}
Gives the interest rate faced by a firm locating in regin \(n\).

Lastly, given a treatment vector \(\mathbb{T}\) and subsidy rate \(s\),
an equilibrium is given by:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A national capital interest rate \(q\)
  \item Local land rents \({r_1, ... r_n}\)
  \item Firm location choice, factor demands and output \({n_i, K_{i, n_i}, L_{i, n_i}, Y_{i, n_i}}_{i \in (0, 1)}\)
\end{itemize}
Such that
\begin{itemize}
  \item Given \(q\) and land rents, firms maximize profits according to their problem;
  \item Land and capital markets clear
\end{itemize}

Substituting prices into demands and output, this yields the \(N+1\) equations system:
\begin{equation}
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
  &D_K(q, r_1, ..., r_n; s, T) - S_K(q) =& 0 \\
  &D_{L_{n=1}}(q, r_1, ..., r_n; s, T) - \bar L_1 =& 0 \\
  .&&\\
  .&&\\
  .&&\\
  &D_{L_{n=N}}(q, r_1, ..., r_n; s, T) - \bar L_N =& 0   
\end{aligned}
\right.
\end{equation}


Operationalization requires:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Moving costs; local productivity - timely proxies available
  \item Capital and land shares - 2007 agricultural census; IDB est. of 0.7 and 0.1 (Labor: 0.2)
  \item Capital supply elasticity - Harder; strong assumptions/parameter sweep may be needed
  \item Program impact on capital - Difficult to estimate due to data; could make assumptions based on program size
  \item Match specific productivity - extreme-value calibration
\end{itemize}
\vspace{1em}

Main possible issues and reasons:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Firm-focused; no welfare outcome - beyond the scope
  \item All land is productive; no role for extensive gains - weak evidence
  \item No labor input - industrial farmwork is not labor intensive
\end{itemize}
Moreover, I wanted to restrict and reduce the model as much as possible
